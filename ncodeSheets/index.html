Dot strengths

Normal, light or extra light

Every dot stays exactly where it is, only its size changes. The pen reads all three, as long as your printer puts the small dots down cleanly. Below: 6 × 6 mm of each, at 600 dpi.

Normal

Identical to the sheets that came with the trick. Use it unless you have a reason not to.

Light

Half the ink, the same way NeoLab makes its own lighter paper. Good when the pattern distracts or prints too dark.

Extra light experimental

The least visible pattern. Works on a colour laser, untested on other printers. Run the strength test first.

Printing guide

Get the dots onto the paper

The pen reads a pattern of tiny dots. Most printing problems come from the printer changing them on the way. These settings prevent that.

In the PDF viewer

Size
“Actual size” / 100 %
Paper
US Letter, or A4 (see Printing on A4). Business cards: A4.
Never
Fit to page, shrink oversized pages, borderless, several pages per sheet, booklet

In the printer settings

Driver
The PostScript one if there is a choice (“PS”, “PS3”, “KPDL”)
Colour
“Black ink only” or “Grayscale”. Not “High quality grayscale”, which mixes grey from all colours.
Quality
The highest setting. 600 or 1200 dpi if you can choose.
Toner saving
Off. It prints the dots too pale or with gaps.
Enhancements
Off. Smoothing, photo optimising and sharpening all change the dots.
Paper type
Plain paper, as smooth and white as you have.

Letter paper has a colour design and needs colour mode. If your colour laser has an option like “Print black as K only” or “Pure black”, switch it on. With inkjet, let the sheet dry before writing on it.

The sheets are US Letter size, 216 × 280 mm. A4 is a little narrower and a little taller. You can print on A4 as long as nothing gets scaled.

Steps

1. Put A4 paper in the printer and choose A4 as the paper size.
2. Set the size to “Actual size” / 100 %. In Adobe Reader untick “Choose paper source by PDF page size”. In Chrome or Edge set Scale to Custom, 100. In Mac Preview set 100 % and untick “Scale to fit”.
3. If the printer reports a paper size mismatch, let it continue with A4.
4. Print the test sheet and measure: 150 mm across, 178 to 182 mm down.

What you get

The page sits in the middle of the A4 sheet. About 3 mm of dots are cut off on the left and right; no design is lost. At the top and bottom you get a white strip of about 8 mm. The app does not see the paper, only the dots, so this is fine. Just don't write right at the edge.

Never let the printer “fit” the page onto A4. That shrinks it by about 3 % and the pen can no longer read the dots.

The dots have to reach the paper exactly as they are in the file. A PostScript driver hands the page over as it is in the PDF and the printer works it out itself. Simple drivers on many cheap printers (“GDI”, “host-based”) turn the page into a picture first and may resize, smooth or toner-save it on the way.

PrinterVerdict
Laser with PostScriptBest choice
Laser without PostScriptMay work. Check with the test sheet
InkjetLeast reliable. Test before printing a stack

Plenty of office and home lasers speak PostScript. Look for a driver called “PS”, “PS3”, “PostScript” or, on Kyocera, “KPDL”.

The pen lights the paper with infrared light, and its camera only sees what swallows that light. Black toner or ink (the K in CMYK) does. Cyan, magenta and yellow let infrared through.

Many colour printers build black out of cyan, magenta and yellow. It looks just as black to you, but to the pen the page is empty. So the dots must come out of the black cartridge. The files store them as pure greyscale black, which printers normally print with black only.

Having the sheets printed professionally? Pass this on.

Dot pattern
600 dpi, pure black and white, no grey levels, no anti-aliasing
Workflow
PostScript / PDF workflow, not an office driver that rasterises on the computer
Page size
216 × 280 mm, business cards on A4
Ink
Carbon black only (K), never rich or four-colour black. Do not convert to CMYK process black.
Resolution
600 or 1200 dpi on the machine
Don'ts
No scaling, no added bleed, no re-screening or image optimising
